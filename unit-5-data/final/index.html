Encompassing Ambiguity: Instagram and Situationships

Situationships - romantic relationships characterized by emotional and physical intimacy without clearly defined labels or commitment, have become increasingly common among college students. This study examined how Instagram use influences situationship dynamics among college students at the University of Mary Washington, with particular attention to how platform interactions such as likes, story views, and direct messages contribute to unclear relationship boundaries. A mixed-methods survey was administered to 22 undergraduate students. Findings indicated that nearly all participants used Instagram behaviors to signal romantic interest, 91% had personal experience with situationships, and 69% believed Instagram interactions probably or definitely contribute to unclear relationship boundaries. These results are consistent with existing literature on communication avoidance and digital romantic signaling and suggest that Instagram may play a meaningful role in sustaining the ambiguity that defines situationship dynamics among college students.

This study set out to examine how Instagram use influences situationship dynamics among college students, with particular attention to how social signals on the platform contribute to unclear relationship boundaries. The findings, drawn from a survey of 22 UMW students, suggest that Instagram is deeply embedded in how college students navigate romantic interest and relationship ambiguity. Nearly all participants reported using platform behaviors to signal romantic interest, the vast majority had personal experience with situationships, and a majority believed that Instagram interactions probably or definitely contribute to unclear relationship boundaries. These findings fit within a broader pattern established in the existing literature. Research consistently shows that situationships are defined by ambiguity and communication avoidance, that young adults are drawn to the intimacy situationships offer while avoiding the vulnerability of commitment, and that social media has made indirect communication the norm for expressing romantic interest. The present study adds to this body of work by providing preliminary evidence that Instagram-specific behaviors, likes, story views, and direct messages, are actively used and interpreted as romantic signals by college students, and that this dynamic may play a role in sustaining the ambiguity that defines situationships.
